# Townsfolk talk to each other

- When two people meet, what they say is now written out: the news that really passes between them, told in their own voice ("Have you heard? My sister lost her job at the bakery. Marta told me."), and answered in the listener's (sympathy, glee, doubt, "That's my brother you're talking about!").
- A story that changed in the retelling starts arguments: "That's not what I heard. I heard it was Tom."
- With no news to share, people talk about the weather, what is on in town this week, the holiday, local lore, work, the family, being tired or hungry, and the place they are in. They talk to their dogs too.
- Children, teenagers and old people sound different, the chatty say more, the formal don't contract, and some people have a pet phrase. American towns say "mom" and "fall".
